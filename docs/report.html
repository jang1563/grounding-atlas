grounding-atlas  /  a measurement-first map

Language models encode far more biology than they can say.

A specialist emits a sequence, a structure, an embedding, a number. Does the language model read its content — or only recognize its name? This project measures the gap, manufactures verifiable signal to close it, and maps where each capability should live.

JangKeun Kim  ·  computational biology, Weill Cornell Medicine (Mason Lab)
single-cell & spatial genomics · space biology · AI evaluation for biology  ·  updated 2026‑07‑02

01

Encoded high, verbalized low

A linear probe on an open model's hidden states recovers the property near a specialist ceiling. The verbalized answer lags far behind — and what sets the gap is not the modality but a capability‑dependent mix of two factors: how familiar the representation's tokens are (reasoning) and how web‑documented its representation‑to‑property mapping is.

Across 17 representations the encoding gap is under 0.10 for 13 of them, while the verbalization gap runs 0.12 to 0.49. The model holds the answer; it just doesn't state it.

The sharpest result is a controlled pair

Two representations with identical task shape, both encoded to the specialist ceiling — yet opposite output. The one thing that differs is whether the representation‑to‑property mapping is web‑documented, isolating the mapping‑documentation factor of the gap.

03

Where each capability should live

If the model already encodes it, don't train it in — read it out. Per capability we measure train (weights) vs retrieve (RAG/MCP) vs orchestrate (call the specialist). The weights lever wins nowhere.

Train the skill

Only where a cheap specialist doesn't already exist. Across every measured cell, a trained read-out is a strong second at best.

Retrieve the knowledge

Closes in-data-pattern and web-anchored cells — few-shot retrieval recovers methylation from 0.40 to 0.93, anchor-invariant.

Orchestrate the specialist

Call the frozen model and route on its output. A thin head on an open SFM beats a learned LLM bridge and in-weight LoRA, and never transfers — reading it out is the win.

The prescription

Because the frontier is calibrated about where it grounds, the same map is a routing policy.

Self-confidence is well-calibrated for knowing when it cannot — it defers the web‑zero tasks — but against real per-item specialists it does not reach the per-item oracle: routed 0.81 vs oracle 0.91, because confidence cannot flag the items where the LLM uniquely beats the specialist. The durable, model-free win is the web-exposure tag, known before any model call: a competitive deferral prior in its own right.

04

“Route, don't train” holds when the model generates

The train-vs-orchestrate line was measured on discriminative read-out. A pre-registered post-training RL environment carries it into the generative regime — and the verdict survives.

A frozen molecular generator, a property reward, a scaffold-disjoint held-out oracle. Two arms at matched reward-query budget: internalized RL (fine-tune the generator toward the reward) versus external guidance (steer the same frozen generator at inference). Which produces more genuine, oracle-confirmed designs?

Across three reward-quality regimes, internalized RL ties external guidance on formal scaffold-clustered CIs. Training the weights buys nothing over routing a frozen prior through the reward — “route, don't train” spans discriminative read-out and generation.

One honest exception, held to its evidence

At high training budget, RL pulls modestly, sub-threshold-ly ahead — it shifts the distribution past the frozen model's guidance ceiling. That edge survived every feasible independence check (an independent-featurization oracle, property and applicability-domain analysis), so it is a genuine, bounded caveat, not an artifact.

05

The line for an AI scientist

A frontier model's job in biology is to ground and route the content a specialist emits — not to know it. Today that grounding is decided by assertion. This project makes it measured.
